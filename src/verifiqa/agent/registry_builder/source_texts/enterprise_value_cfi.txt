Source: Corporate Finance Institute (CFI)
URL: https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/valuation/what-is-enterprise-value/
Authority: CFA Institute, Equity Investments curriculum
Metric: Enterprise Value (EV)

PRIMARY FORMULA:
EV = Market Capitalization + Market Value of Debt - Cash and Equivalents

EXTENDED FORMULA:
EV = Common Shares + Preferred Shares + Market Value of Debt + Noncontrolling Interest - Cash and Equivalents

VARIABLE DEFINITIONS:
- Market Capitalization: Fully-diluted shares outstanding multiplied by current stock price
- Market Value of Debt: Short-term and long-term interest-bearing liabilities
- Preferred Shares: Hybrid securities with fixed dividends and priority claims
- Noncontrolling Interest: Portion of a subsidiary not owned by the parent company
- Cash and Cash Equivalents: Subtracted as it could immediately reduce debt

VERBATIM FORMULA STATEMENTS:
"EV = Market Capitalization + Market Value of Debt – Cash and Equivalents"
"Enterprise Value (EV) is the measure of a company's total value. It looks at the entire market value rather than just the equity value, so all ownership interests and asset claims from both debt and equity are included."
