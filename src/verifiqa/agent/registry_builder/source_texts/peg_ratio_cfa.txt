Source: CFA Institute, Equity Investments curriculum
Authority: CFA Institute Program Curriculum, Level 1, Equity Valuation
Metric: PEG Ratio (Price/Earnings to Growth)

PRIMARY FORMULA:
PEG = P/E Ratio / Earnings Growth Rate

ALTERNATIVE FORMULA:
PEG = (Price Per Share / Earnings Per Share) / Annual EPS Growth Rate

VARIABLE DEFINITIONS:
- P/E Ratio: Price per share divided by earnings per share
- Earnings Growth Rate: Expected annual EPS growth rate, expressed as a percentage (not a decimal)
- Result: A PEG below 1.0 may indicate undervaluation relative to growth; above 1.0 may indicate overvaluation

VERBATIM FORMULA STATEMENTS:
"PEG Ratio = P/E Ratio / Earnings Growth Rate"
"The PEG ratio adjusts the P/E ratio by the company's expected earnings growth rate to account for the fact that high-growth companies deserve higher P/E multiples."
"A PEG ratio of 1.0 is often considered fairly valued."

KEY NOTES:
Developed by Peter Lynch. Growth rate is typically the expected 5-year EPS CAGR.
Sensitive to growth rate estimate — a small change in expected growth materially changes the PEG.
