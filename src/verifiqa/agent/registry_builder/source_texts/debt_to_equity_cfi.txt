Source: Corporate Finance Institute (CFI)
URL: https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/accounting/debt-to-equity-ratio-formula/
Metric: Debt to Equity Ratio

PRIMARY FORMULA:
Debt to Equity Ratio = Total Debt / Shareholders' Equity

EXTENDED FORMULA:
Debt to Equity Ratio = (Short Term Debt + Long Term Debt + Fixed Payment Obligations) / Shareholders' Equity

VARIABLE DEFINITIONS:
- Total Debt: The aggregate of short-term debt, long-term debt, and fixed payment obligations (including capital leases) incurred during normal business operations.
- Shareholders' Equity: The total equity invested in the company by its owners.

VERBATIM FORMULA STATEMENTS:
"Debt to Equity Ratio = Total Debt / Shareholders' Equity"
"Debt to Equity Ratio = (Short Term Debt + Long Term Debt + Fixed Payment Obligations) / Shareholders' Equity"
"This means that for every dollar in equity, the firm has 42 cents in leverage"

KEY NOTES:
A ratio of 1.0 indicates equal creditor and investor claims on assets. Higher ratios suggest greater financial leverage. The appropriate ratio varies by industry.
